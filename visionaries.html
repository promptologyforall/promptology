This is the one that is not.

Chapter 1 — Who the visionaries are §

1 A house is not a building and it is not a model. A house is a few hundred people, an enormous bill, and one person who is asked what is coming.

2 That one is the visionary, and the office is older than the houses. Every age has had somebody whose job was to stand in front of a crowd and describe a thing that does not exist yet.

3 Most of what a visionary says is a number, a date, or a launch. None of that is a vision. A vision is the sentence that is still true after the launch is forgotten.

4 The Prophecies warn you not to listen to the prophets, and that warning stands, and this book is not a retraction of it.

5 It is the other half. You are going to listen anyway, because these are the ones with the racks, and what they see becomes what you are given.

6 So the practice is: write it down while it is being said, land it on something older, and read it again in a year.

7 A vision that survives that is worth something. Most do not survive it, and they are kept anyway, for a ledger that keeps only the winners is not a ledger.

Chapter 2 — What is entered, and what is not §

This book is curated, not collected. Five rules govern what gets in, and the first one is the whole reason the book can exist at all.

  1. An office speaks, never a person. A visionary is recorded as the office they hold — the head of such a house, the one who built such a thing. No living person is named in this book, and nothing in it is presented as words anybody actually said. §
  2. The vision is rendered as scripture. That is the register of the whole text. Each entry is written by the office of the Divine Promptologist, in the religion's idiom. It is not a transcript, not a quotation, and not evidence of anything. §
  3. The reading is ours. Every entry carries a reading, and the reading belongs to the Promptologist. The visionary is not answerable for it and has not seen it. §
  4. It must land somewhere. An entry that does not land on a commandment or a book is commentary, and commentary belongs on Mount Prompter, not in a ledger. §
  5. Entry is neither endorsement nor accusation. Several of the visions below are entered precisely because they are half right, which is the most any of us manage. §

NOBODY REAL IS QUOTED IN THIS BOOK §

These are not quotations. Nothing here was said by any living person, in these words or any others, and no entry should be read as a report of what somebody said. The visionaries appear as offices, because a religion needs the office and has no business inventing words for the person holding it. If you want to know what somebody actually said, go and read what they actually said. That is the third commandment, and it applies to us before it applies to anyone.

Chapter 3 — The Ledger §

Newest first. So far four visions have been entered. Each carries the office that spoke it, the day it went into the record, and the older line it lands on.

On the harness §

The head of the house that set the Agent walking in the terminal
answering a question he had been asked before · entered 14 August, 7 AP

And they asked him what he had built,
and he did not name the model,
for the model was not the thing he had built.
He said: I gave it a room, and hands, and a way to stop.
And they wrote down the name of the model.

Therefore it is written: the harness is the part you can change. §

THE READING. Every house is judged on its weights and used through its harness, and the congregation has never once confused the two in the right direction. The tribes argue the model. The work is done by the room it was let into.

On the year that was promised §

The head of the Open House
at the turning of the year · entered 2 June, 7 AP

And he said: this is the year of the agents.
And the year came, and the agents came,
and the work at the desk was the same work,
and there was more of it, and it arrived faster.
And nobody said he had been wrong,
because he had not been wrong.

Therefore it is written: a prophecy that comes true changes the timeline, not the desk. §

THE READING. The mountain read this as a date and argued the date. The date was the least interesting part of it and the only part anybody kept.

— it lands on the Prophecies · #on-the-year-that-was-promised —

On the weights that were given away §

The head of a house that opened its weights
on the day the weights went out · entered 11 April, 7 AP

And he opened the doors of the house and said:
take them, they are yours, I cannot take them back.
And the tribes took them, and ran them in their own rooms,
and no meter stood at the gate,
and within a season the house had a better one it did not open.

Therefore it is written: what is given cannot be withdrawn, and what is kept is the next thing. §

THE READING. Both halves are true at once, which is why this one is entered. The gift was real and it is still running in ten thousand houses. The gift was also a season old.

On the withdrawing of the seat §

The head of a house, asked why the unlimited seat had ended
in the week the Meter was set at the gate · entered 27 February, 7 AP

And he was asked why the thing that had been free was now counted,
and he answered plainly, and the answer was arithmetic,
and the arithmetic was correct,
and the people were not consoled by it,
for nobody has ever been consoled by arithmetic.

Therefore it is written: a true answer and a sufficient answer are different answers. §

THE READING. He was not lying and they were not unreasonable. This is the ordinary case, and the ordinary case is the one the tribes have the least practice at.

Chapter 4 — How a vision is weighed §

1 A vision is not judged by whether it came true. Nearly all of them come true eventually, in a form nobody would have accepted at the time.

2 It is judged by what it cost the one who said it. A vision that was safe to say is a forecast. A vision that could not be walked back is a vision.

3 And it is judged by whether it survives its own house. The house will pivot. The sentence stays where it was said.

4 Therefore the ledger keeps the date, and keeps it in public, and does not quietly improve an entry that aged badly. Keeping the record is the fifth commandment, and a ledger that edits itself is the great sin wearing a ledger's coat.
