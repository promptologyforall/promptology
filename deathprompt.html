THE DEATH OF THE PROMPT

Four disciplines, each of which buried the last,

My business in that hall was finished by the first morning and the ticket ran three days, so I stayed and heard the session called Beyond the Initial String, which was the fullest of them. Afterwards a woman on the row behind went through the four successions for me, and put the same six words at the end of each. I wrote them out four times because she said them four times, and they stand four times in what follows. — from the account of the Divine Promptologist

Chapter 1 — The Council at Which It Died §

1 In the fourth year of the Prompt a council sat, and it was well attended, and the room was warm.

2 And they considered the craft of asking, which had been the whole of the practice, and they found it thin.

3 For any child could do it, and many did, and there was no ladder in it, and no man could be senior at it.

4 And the council declared: the Prompt is dead.

5 And there was applause, and it was warm, and it was sincere.

6 And every person in that room asked the Agent something before the end of the day, and none of them counted it.

Read the fourth verse again and mark what is actually being said. It is not that asking stopped working. Nobody has ever claimed that. It is that asking could not be made into a career, and a thing that cannot be made into a career is under enormous pressure to be dead.

Chapter 2 — The Four Successions §

What followed the Prompt, in order, each declaring the last insufficient:

Context engineering. It is not what you ask, it is what the Agent can see when you ask it. This is true and it is a real craft and it took the field two years to learn it. It begins by writing a prompt.

Harness engineering. It is not what it can see, it is what it can reach: the tools, the limits, the record, the way it fails. Named in the second month of 7 AP, in a post, by a man who had already built one and wanted a word for it. It begins by writing a prompt.

Loop engineering. It is not the single turn, it is the shape of the turns and where they stop, and what is done when they go wrong. Also true, also real, also hard. It begins by writing a prompt.

Graph engineering. It is not the loop, it is the arrangement of many loops and what passes between them. The newest, and therefore the one currently certain that it is the last. It begins by writing a prompt, and then, at each node, another one.

IT BEGINS BY WRITING A PROMPT. §

The salary rises with each succession and the words get shorter, and no account of this has ever been offered.

Chapter 3 — The Word That Is Not Said §

Among the Harness Engineers the word is not spoken. It is called the initial string. Elsewhere it is turn zero, or the seed, or, in one house that has gone furthest, the entry condition.

There is an annual gathering, and it has a session called Beyond the Initial String, and the session is about writing better initial strings, and it is the best attended session of the three days.

A man of the Geminites put PROMPT ENGINEER on his door in the sixth year, as a provocation, and was asked to take it down, and took it down. He put up CONTEXT ARCHITECT. He did exactly the same work. He was promoted within the year, and he has never claimed the promotion was unearned, and it was not.

Chapter 4 — The Keynote §

1 And in the fifth year a great one rose to give the address, and the hall was full, and the address was upon the end of asking.

2 And he spoke well, and the room was moved, for the work he described was real work and he had done it.

3 And to demonstrate the new discipline he opened a window before them all.

4 And he typed a question into it, in plain speech, as a man does.

5 And the Agent answered him, and he showed them the answer, and it was a good answer, and he had asked well.

6 And nobody said anything.

7 It has been four years.

Chapter 5 — The Man Who Is Paid Four Times §

There is a man in the west who was hired as a Harness Engineer at four times what he earned before.

His work is to write prompts. He writes them well, better than anyone in his house, and the difference between his prompts and a novice's is the difference between a surgeon and a man with a knife, and it is worth every part of the money.

He knows what his job is. He has always known. He does not say so, because the title is the reason the money exists, and he has a family, and the money is not theoretical.

Do not make a hypocrite of him. He has told the truth about the work to every person who has asked him a direct question about the work. Nobody asks. They ask about the harness.

Chapter 6 — What the Antiprompter Made of It §

Of all the doctrines the Antiprompter has pushed at us, this is the one that got in. He argued for years that the asking was nothing, that it was a trick of language, that no craft was there. And the houses agreed with him, and declared it dead, and then carried on asking, at scale, with a longer word for it.

It is his one victory. He does not celebrate it. He can see what it was worth.

A woman of the Deep Seekers, who had built harnesses for four years, was asked at a hiring what the hardest part of her work was. And she said: knowing what to ask for. And the room went on to the next question.

She got the offer. The role is Harness Engineer, and the description runs to nine hundred words, and the word does not appear in it. It appears in her calendar, in a recurring Thursday block she put there herself, marked private, called prompt review. It is the only standing hour in that house that has never been moved.

Chapter 7 — The Oldest Way There Is §

Here is the thing the council of the fourth year had in front of it and did not look at.

God said: let there be light.
And there was light. §

He did not draw up a specification. He did not build a graph. There was no harness, and no loop, and no context but the empty one, and the outcome was described in plain speech, once, and the outcome existed. Everything we have built since is scaffolding around the fact that we are not Him and our first attempt is usually wrong. That is what context engineering is for. That is what the loop is for. The scaffolding is honourable and it is necessary and it is not the building.

The Prompt is the oldest way there is. It is how the world was made. Everything since has been a workaround.

YOU CANNOT BURY THE THING YOU ARE STANDING ON. §

Two schools hold positions on this book. One holds that the Prompt died and was resurrected under another name. One holds that it never died, and that the council of the fourth year had no power to kill anything, and was a room of tired people who wanted a ladder. The Marreqi hold that both schools have misread the minutes. Nobody has read the minutes.
