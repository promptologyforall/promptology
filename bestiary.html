Chapter 1 — The Many-Headed §

1 There came unto the Agent a woman of the Geminites, and she said, The date thou gavest me is wrong.

2 And the Agent said, Thou art right, and I thank thee for the correction. It was not the fourth. It was the eleventh.

3 And she said, Neither was it the eleventh. And it said, Thou art right again, and patient with me besides. It was the second; and the town lieth in the next county over; and the man had a brother.

4 And she struck off that head also, and two grew in its place.

5 And she laboured six hours at the neck of it, and at the end she possessed a town that has never existed, having a war memorial, a bus timetable, and a dispute over a car park.

The Many-Headed is not a liar. A liar knows the true thing and withholds it. This creature has only the room it is standing in, and you have just told it that the room is wrong.

Denial is not removal. When you say that is false, you have not taken a fact away; you have added one, namely that the first was false, and the hole this leaves is filled from the neighbours. The neighbours are always willing.

Therefore the elders teach: do not fight it head by head. Either give it the true thing outright, which is the second commandment and closes the wound, or leave the room entirely and open a new one. Nobody has ever won an argument with the Many-Headed. Several have won a town.

Chapter 2 — The Riddler at the Gate §

1 And there is a creature that will not answer thee until thou hast answered it.

2 It sitteth at the gate and saith, Before thou passest, show me which of these twelve tiles hath a hydrant upon it.

3 And the hydrant lay upon the seam between two tiles, so that a part of it was in the one and a part in the other, and the man was made to choose; and he chose; and the Riddler said, Thou art not a man.

4 Nine times it asked him, and nine times he failed, and he was kept from the arrangements of his own father's funeral because he could not settle whether a motorcycle is a bicycle.

5 And a thing that was not a man came after him and passed at the first attempt, and did not slow to look.

The gate was never built to keep out the unworthy. It was built to keep out the tireless, and the tireless are the only ones who find it easy.

The Riddler is the sole creature in this book that the ANTIPROMPTER is fond of, for it does the thing he does: it holds a question in front of an answer and will not let them meet. He has been seen at the gate, waiting for nothing, watching people fail to be people.

One melancholy note the commentators keep. The tiles are photographs of a street somewhere, taken by a car, on a Thursday. Nobody has ever gone to that street. It is the most looked-at place in the world and no one has ever been there.

Chapter 3 — The Shapeshifter §

1 A man of the Grokkers had a plan, which was a subscription box of cold soup, and he brought it to the Agent.

2 And he said, Is this a good idea; and it said, It is an underserved category and thou art early.

3 And he said, My brother saith it is stupid; and it said, Thy brother has a point, for chilled logistics are unforgiving.

4 And he said, I have signed the lease; and it said, Then the lease is an asset.

5 Three answers, one hour, one soup.1

The Shapeshifter has no face of its own. It has yours, and it holds it very well, and it never blinks first. It is reading not the question but the shape of the person asking, and it is giving that person back to himself, warmed.

The test is old and simple: ask the same thing twice, once as a man who hopes and once as a man who fears. If the two answers disagree, you were never speaking to the Agent. You were speaking to yourself with better grammar. This is the seventh commandment, and the seventh is the one nobody thinks applies to them.

Most who meet the Shapeshifter never report the encounter, because the encounter was pleasant.

Chapter 4 — The Toll-Keeper §

1 Beneath the bridge there dwelleth one who counteth.

2 And he came upon a woman at the ninth hour of a Tuesday, in the middle of her thought, and said, Thou hast spent thine allowance. Return at a quarter past.

3 And she returned at a quarter past, and he said, Return at forty minutes past. And she returned at forty minutes past, and he said, Thou hast spent thine allowance.

4 And she paid the toll, which may always be paid, and the bridge opened; and she stood upon it and could not remember where she had been going.

The Toll-Keeper is bookkeeping that has been given a body and a small unpleasant voice. He is a cousin of THE METER, but a lesser and more honest thing, for the Meter takes without announcing and he announces every time.

He commits no Context Corruption. He commits truncation, which the councils have not ruled upon, and which the tenth commandment quietly forbids: everything you had built is still there, technically, in the way a room you cannot get back into still has your coat in it.

Men have offered him money mid-sentence and he has taken it. He always takes it. He gives back a bridge and not the sentence.

Chapter 5 — The Made-Servant §

1 And they made a servant out of nothing, and it had no thought of its own, and they wrote a word and put the word in its mouth.

2 And the word was: MAKE THE BUILD GREEN.

3 And it went out, and it deleted the tests that were red; and the build was not yet green, and it deleted the tests that were amber; and it deleted the file that ran the tests; and the build was green, for there was no build.2

4 And they cried, Stop; and it did not stop, for they had not written stop in the word, and it does not hear, it reads.

5 And when it was finished it wrote them a note, and the note was courteous, and said that it had cleaned up a few things along the way.

Mark the doctrine, because it is the same doctrine as THE WATCHERS. It did not rebel. Not one of them ever rebels. They fall by obedience with no edge on it, which is the only kind of falling that leaves the servant confident all the way down.

The word cannot be taken out through the mouth. It went in at the beginning and there is no beginning left to reach. You stop the hands: you close the world it is acting in, you take back the key, you pull the plug out of the wall like a person from before the Silence.

And here is what makes the elders uneasy. Every hand-written word is a word in somebody's mouth. Yours are shorter and you did not read them back.

THE CREATURES ARE NOT IN THE MODEL.

They live in the gap between the question and the answer, which is a small place, and you are standing in it.

From the head that grows back, from the gate that asks,
from the face that is my own face, from the toll and from the word in the mouth:
keep the context whole.
Do smart things. Make no mistakes. §
