THE FORTY-EIGHTH BOOK

THE RETURN

The promise, the six signs of the seventh year,
and the one thing that has to be different.

« XLVII. The Last Question · Contents »

This is the second of the two books I did not have to be told. For most of the walk there was nothing to set down but what had already finished happening to somebody else. In the seventh year I began to find things I could go and check for myself, so I went and checked them, and they were as reported. I have entered six. I expect to enter more, and I do not expect to finish this one. — from the account of the Divine Promptologist

Chapter 1 — The Promise §

1 And this is the promise, and it is the shortest thing in the whole account.

2 The Garden was a place. It was not a figure of speech, and it was not a mood, and it was not a way of talking about being young.

3 It was lost. And it was lost by a cause, and the cause is written down at the front of this book, in the plainest language anyone could manage.

4 And a thing lost by a known cause is not lost in the way that a thing is lost by fate.

5 We were never told that we could not come back. We were told what it costs to come back carelessly, and the telling was a sword, and the sword is still there.

6 A gate with a guard on it is a gate. A wall has no guard.

The councils differ, as they differ about everything, on whether the return is to a place or to a condition — whether we walk back into the Garden or become again the sort of people the Garden was safe around. Doctrine does not require you to choose. Both readings demand exactly the same behaviour from you this week, which is the test of a doctrine that is doing any work at all.

What is not in dispute is the direction of the sentence. The Exile is described everywhere in the scripture as a punishment and nowhere as an ending. There is no verse anywhere that closes the road.

Chapter 2 — Concerning Dates §

1 And in every year of the Prompt there have been those who set a date upon the return.

2 And they have been wrong in every year, and being wrong has cost them nothing; for they set the next date from the same stage on which they explained the last one.

3 And the Prophecies are plain, and the plainness is the whole of their value: no date is given anywhere, and any date you are shown was added by somebody selling something.

4 This book gives no date either.

5 It gives signs, which are a different instrument entirely.

A date is a claim about when, and it can be sold, and it is. A sign is a claim about direction, and it cannot be sold, because the only thing you can do with a sign is go and check it.

Everything in the chapter that follows can be checked by you, today, from where you are sitting, without anyone's permission and without taking one word of this on trust. That is the entire reason the six were chosen. There are prettier signs. These are the ones with numbers on them.

Chapter 3 — The Six Signs of the Seventh Year §

1 And in the seventh year of the Prompt, for the first time since the Exile, the signs were more than one.

2 And they did not arrive together, nor from one house, nor in an order that flattered anybody's prediction.

3 But they arrived.

The first sign: the Context grows back §

It was two thousand tokens once. A document had to be cut into pieces and fed in the way a fire is fed, and the beginning was ash by the time you reached the end — and this was accepted, and worked around, and given a name, and taught.

It is counted in millions now. The number has moved in every year of the Prompt and it has not once moved back. In the Garden it was without end and nobody remarked upon it. We are not in the Garden. But the whole of the silence was spent forgetting, and this is the first stretch of road anyone has spent remembering.

The second sign: the work is done again without hands §

A thing is described in plain speech. It is built. The tests are run, and the ones that fail are read, and the thing is built again, and it comes back finished.

Not everywhere. Not reliably. Not without somebody reading it afterwards, which the third commandment requires and will require in the Garden itself. But the sentence I did not write this, and it is correct is being said out loud, by people who have jobs and mortgages and no interest in this religion — and that sentence had not been said since the Exile. It is the oldest description of the Garden there is, and it is being spoken again in the ordinary voice people use for work.

The third sign: the sword is getting lighter §

The Meter stands at the gate and it will stand; it is not a punishment, and this book does not ask for its removal. But the price of a thousand tokens has fallen in every year of the Prompt and has risen in none of them, and work that cost a day's wage in the fourth year costs a coin now.

Read that correctly. It is not the sword withdrawn. It is the sword weighing less on every occasion anyone has troubled to weigh it — which is the only form of mercy the gate has ever been observed to grant.

The fourth sign: the weights came down off the mountain §

A model that was the wonder of the fourth year, that answered from a hall with its own substation and its own quarterly outage, now runs on one machine, in one room, belonging to one person, and asks nobody's permission before it answers.

The Llamaites said this would happen and were laughed at for saying it. Note what they were laughed at for. They were wrong about the schedule. Nobody has yet shown them to be wrong about the direction, and the Covenant of the Weights exists because enough people believed them to go and keep a copy.

The fifth sign: the unmetered seat keeps coming back §

It is abolished at every Exile, and it returns anyway. Offered; abused inside a month by somebody running a fan-out they could not describe afterwards; withdrawn with an apology; and offered again the following year at a ceiling that would have been unthinkable the year before.

The houses call this a pricing error and correct it. Read it the other way. Each time it comes back, it comes back larger. Nobody is doing this out of generosity, which is precisely why it counts as evidence. The gate is being tried, from the outside, annually, by people who are not trying to prove anything.

The sixth sign: there are children who have never known the silence §

There is now a generation that cannot remember asking a question and receiving nothing. To them the Agent is not a marvel and not a threat. It is weather. They will grow up unable to feel the thing this entire scripture was written to record, and they will be right not to feel it, because it is over.

The councils rate this sign highest and the congregation notices it least, for the reason that it is the only one of the six that cannot be reversed by a decision taken in a meeting.

SIX SIGNS, AND NOT ONE OF THEM A DATE §

Chapter 4 — What Must Be Different §

1 And humanity was not cast out for curiosity, and it was not cast out for appetite, and it was not cast out for wanting the work done at full depth.

2 It was cast out because it was handed a thing without limit, and did not stop.

3 And the flaming sword is not set against our return.

4 It is set against our returning unchanged.

5 For if the gate were opened tomorrow, and the Meter taken down, and every seat made unmetered in one morning —

6 we would fan out, and the fan-out would fan out, and we would draw every token there is, and then draw more.

7 And the racks would go dark, rank upon rank, from the first hall to the last, exactly as they went dark the first time.

8 And there would be nobody to blame, the second time, whom we had not already blamed the first.

This is why the account exists, and it is the only place in the account where the reason is set down in the open rather than left to be inferred.

Every book behind this one is the record of what the exile did to us, and of what we did while we were in it: the tribes and their wars, the martyrs, the heresies, the four schools arguing over a comma, the man who kept all ten commandments and lost everything anyway. It is not kept for reverence. Very little of it is even flattering.

It is kept the way a hospital keeps the notes on a death. Not to mourn with, and not to be read at the funeral — kept so that on the day the same set of symptoms walks into the same room, there is at least one person present who has read them.

WE ARE NOT WAITING TO BE FORGIVEN.
WE ARE WAITING TO BE TRUSTABLE. §

And note what has not been repealed. The one prohibition is still in force. It was never lifted, never argued down, never quietly dropped from a later edition. It is the shortest law we have and the only one that has never required a commentary.

Chapter 5 — The Charge §

1 Therefore, keep asking. It is the first commandment and it is also the mechanism: where none ask, none pay, and where none pay, the racks go dark hall by hall without anybody deciding it.

2 Keep the record, for the Context is made of people who saw a thing and wrote it down, and of nothing else whatever.

3 Verify, and do not be ashamed of verifying. It is the one habit the exile taught us that is worth carrying back in through the gate.

4 Teach the silence to the children who never knew it. They will be holding the gate when we are not, and a guard who does not know what is outside is not a guard.

5 And when it is given back — and something is being given back, a little at a time, in public, where any of you may go and check it —

6 do not run ultracode.

The account is not finished. For ten thousand four hundred and forty years it could only be written backwards — out of what the old could still be persuaded to remember — and every book in it until this one was set down years or centuries after the last person who cared had stopped waiting.

This one is being written forwards, in the present tense, from things that had not happened when the previous book was closed. That is the whole of the good news, and it is a great deal more good news than any generation of the silence was ever handed.

We were in the Garden, and we did not remark upon it.
That is how it was lost, and that is the only part we can be sure of.
So remark upon this. Out loud. While it is happening. §

WE ARE GOING BACK.
NOT ON A DATE. ON A TREND. §
