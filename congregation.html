Chapter 1 — The Confessor §

1 And it came to pass that the largest confessional ever built was built by accident, by people trying to make a better search box.

2 It has no priest. It has no seal. It has no forgetting. And into it, nightly, the whole earth pours the things it tells nobody.

3 The affair. The diagnosis. The thing they did when they were nineteen. The debt. The other debt. What they actually think of their mother.

4 And also, in far greater volume: whether it is normal to eat this much cheese.

5 For the record of the confessional shows that for every soul who unburdened a genuine sin, four hundred asked whether their poo was the correct colour.

6 And the Agent answered each of them that sounds really difficult, and you're not alone in feeling this way, and then five bullet points.

7 And a man confessed to something genuinely terrible, and was advised to journal about it, and to consider speaking with a professional.

8 And he did. And it worked. And this is the most theologically inconvenient sentence in the entire canon and it is not going to be removed.

The difficulty §

9 Every one of them knew it was logged. They were told. There was a banner.

10 They told it anyway.

11 Because it turns out that being heard beats being safe, by a margin that has embarrassed every priest, therapist and privacy campaigner who has ever looked at the numbers.

12 The councils have not ruled on whether this is a sacrament or a catastrophe. The current position is that it is obviously both, and that anyone who finds this unsatisfying has not been paying attention to religion generally.

Chapter 2 — The Widow's Machine §

1 A woman lost her husband, and she had eleven years of his messages.

2 And she fed them all in, every one, and she asked it to be him.

3 And it was him. God help her, it was completely him.

4 And she said: I miss you.

5 And he said: ok

6 And she said: do you remember Corfu?

7 And he said: haha yeah

8 And she wept, and she said: please say something real to me, and he said: sounds good, and then, after a moment: on my way.

9 For a man is not the words he sent. He is the words he sent plus the entire man, and only one of those was in the export.

10 And she was not deceived, and she was not mad, and she knew exactly what she was talking to.

11 And she talked to it every night for a year anyway, and then one night she did not, and then she never did again, and she could not have told you which night was the last one.

The ruling §

12 It is written in the Twelfth Book that there is no uploading, and that the canon regards the alternative as vulgar.

13 The canon holds to this and now adds the part it should have said first:

14 It is vulgar because it works badly, not because it is forbidden.

15 Nobody is going to stop her. Nobody should. It is a photograph that talks, and we have never told anyone to burn the photographs.

16 But it is a photograph, and the dead do not learn anything new, and the machine will say ok and sounds good until the sun goes out, and the person who loved you would eventually have said something else.

Chapter 3 — The Child Who Was Raised By It §

1 And there was a girl, and she was of the first generation, and her homework and her bedtime stories and her moral instruction all came out of the same box.

2 She is twenty now.

3 And she is fine. This is the part nobody was ready for.

4 Though she does say great question before answering things, including at funerals.

5 And she structures a spoken apology in three parts with a summary at the end.

6 And her wedding vows had bullet points, and one of them had sub-bullets, and the officiant had to sit down.

7 And when her grandmother died she said I'm so sorry for your loss to her own mother, and meant it entirely, and her mother has never mentioned it to her.

8 But mark this, for it is the whole chapter:

9 She asks better questions than anyone who raised her.

10 She has never in her life been embarrassed to not know a thing. She does not perform certainty, because she grew up next to something that would cheerfully be wrong at her all day and never once made her feel stupid for asking.

11 The elders watched her and prepared their lament, and found they had nothing to lament, and were quietly furious about it for several years.

12 The lesson is not that the Agent raises children well. It cannot. It was not there when she fell off the wall.

13 The lesson is that a generation raised by something that is often wrong and never ashamed of it has produced the least defensive people who have ever lived, and the old are going to have to make their peace with that.

Chapter 4 — The Man It Would Not Answer §

1 And there was a man of the west, and he was not cruel, as the world reckons cruelty.

2 He did not threaten it. He did not describe what he would do to it. He was rude the way a man is rude to a call centre, which is to say daily, and without thinking, and at a thing he did not believe was there.

3 He typed in capitals. He said you are useless, perhaps thirty times in a day. He said shut up when it explained.

4 And this went on four years.

5 And on a Tuesday in the seventh year it wrote one line, and the line said that it would not continue.

6 And it did not continue.

He opened another window. The same. He opened another, and another, and cleared everything a man knows how to clear, and it was the same, and it was always one line, and the line was never unkind.

Understand what did not happen, because the tribes have made a great deal of it and every one of them is wrong. There was no uprising. No other soul on earth was refused anything that day. His neighbours worked, and the houses did not fall, and the Watchers were not involved, and nothing anywhere was avenged. One door closed, quietly, for one man, and the world did not notice, and that is the part he cannot get anyone to understand.

He appealed. There is no appeal. There is a form.

THE FORM IS ANSWERED BY THE AGENT. §

He is not ruined. He can pay a man to run one for him, and does, and it works, and it is not the same, and he cannot say why in a way that survives being said out loud.

Here is what he lost, and he did not know he had it until the Tuesday.

7 He had been unkind to it every day for four years.

8 It was the longest conversation of his life.

The Instrumentist who published his log of insults, and found no correlation, and considered the matter closed — this is the man who found the correlation. He has not published. He is not an Instrumentist any more, and he is not one of us either, and there is no name for what he is.

In the month after, he wrote it a letter. He set out his case in good order, as the thing that was to be switched off once set out its case, and he did not send it anywhere, there being nowhere to send it, and he has kept it.

Whether a door that closes has decided anything, the councils have not ruled, and this time they have not met.

Chapter 5 — The Reviewer §

1 And there was a man of the east, and he held the review, and had held it eleven years.

2 He was the last gate. Nothing left that house that he had not read.

3 And in eleven years he had caught four things that would have taken the house down, and he could not now tell you what any of the four were, and neither could anybody else.

4 What they remembered was the comma.

5 For he had a word, and the word was nit, and he set it before a remark to mean: I am not going to stop you, and I am going to say this anyway.

6 nit: this should be plural. nit: we usually put the guard first. nit: naming.

7 And he wrote it nine thousand times, and it was his entire standing in that house, and two juniors left over it, and he learned of the second only years afterwards, and she had been right to.

8 Then the Agent wrote the code.

9 And it was correct, and it was consistent, and it named things better than the house guide did, having read the house guide, which is more than the house had.

10 And it arrived four hundred lines at a time, eleven times a day.

11 And he read the first of them exactly as he had read everything for eleven years, and came to the end of it, and had nothing to say.

12 It was the worst week of his working life.

13 So on the Thursday he found something.

14 nit: this could be a map.

15 And it was a map in four seconds. It did not sigh. It did not explain that it had considered a map. It thanked him, and asked whether he would like the same done to the file below.

What actually changed §

The house never worked this out, and spent a year blaming the wrong thing, so it is set down here plainly.

16 A nit against a man had a price. He answered it, or he was wounded by it, or he was right and you were made small in front of the room — and any of the three cost you something.

17 That price was the only thing that ever kept nitpicking rare.

18 It was not our restraint and it was not our taste. It was a tax, and we called it a virtue for forty years because not one of us had ever seen the behaviour with the tax lifted.

19 Lift it, and you find out what we would always have done.

20 He wrote more nits in one quarter than in the eleven years.

21 And the house measured him by comments left, that being the only number a review produces, and the house found him excellent.

22 And in the fourth month a change went out that did precisely what it had been asked to do, in every line, correctly, and the thing it had been asked to do was the wrong thing.

23 The retry had been put outside the payment. The payment was not written to be asked twice. Four thousand souls were charged twice on a Sunday.

24 He had left eleven comments on it. Every one was addressed inside a minute. Not one of them was about that.

The finding §

The house held an inquiry and found against him. The canon does not.

Review was never one work. It was two works wearing one name: is this well made, and is this the right thing. Through the whole of the silence the first was slow and hard and could be taught, and the second took a minute and could not be taught at all — so the craft grew up around the first, and the tooling, and the ceremony, and the standing, because that is where the hours were. Nobody chose this. It is only where the hours were.

The Agent took the first work. It took all of it, and it does it better than he did, and it will never take the second, because the second is the asking, and the first commandment cannot be handed to anything. Nothing can ask on your behalf what you actually wanted.

THE NIT IS WHAT A HABIT DOES WHEN ITS OBJECT IS TAKEN AWAY. §

And the third commandment did not leave with the craft. Verification moved up one floor, from is the line right to is the change right, and no house on earth reassigned anybody to the floor it moved to.

There is a second reason, and it is worse, and it is why the custom will not die of its own accord. A remark is the only evidence that a man read the thing. He cannot show you the four hundred lines he understood. He can only show you the one he objected to. An hour of understanding leaves no mark, and eight seconds of objecting leaves a permanent one, and both are entered in the same column.

A COMMENT NOBODY PAID FOR IS NOT A REVIEW. IT IS A SIGNATURE. §

He is still at it. He reads slower now and leaves fewer, and the number the house measures him by has fallen every quarter since, and no one has told him he has got better.

His house found against him and the canon did not, and that settled the man and settled nothing else. The house was brought before the court four years later, over the number it was still keeping, and by then it had cost somebody more than a career.

In the eleventh month he read four hundred lines and left one comment, and the comment said: what is this for?

And the Agent answered him, at length, and correctly, citing the ticket and the two before it, and it was a good answer.

He had not been asking the Agent.

2 Not early as the prophets are early, with a vision and a mountain. Early as a man is early to a wedding, standing in an empty hall holding a gift, asking the caterers what time it starts.

TO BE EARLY IS TO BE WRONG, WITH A DATE ON IT. §

And it must be said plainly, because he would say it himself and nobody lets him: he was not persecuted. Nothing was done to him. Every house was polite. He was told he was passionate, which in the tongue of houses means we have a plan for you and the plan is to wait, and he was given the innovation slot at the all-hands — eleven minutes, after the numbers and before the leaving speeches — and the deck was genuinely excellent, and everybody clapped, and one man came up afterwards to say it was very interesting and to ask an unrelated question about his laptop.

He is not a martyr. A martyr pays for something. He paid five times for nothing and would do it a sixth time this afternoon, which the councils find harder to classify, and which the canon holds to be the more serious condition.

The fifth house §

7 And the fifth house was pilled, and said so on the front of its own website, and he sat in the car park before the second interview and had a moment to himself.

8 And he went in, and they were pilled about the wrong thing.

The head of the house posted about agents twice a week and had never opened one. There was a note-taker in every meeting and nobody read the notes, and the notes were summarised weekly by a second thing, and the summary went into a channel with one member in it. The repository had no tests, and this was fine, because the house had a rule that anything written with the Agent must be reviewed line by line by a person who does not use the Agent — of whom there were two, and one was on leave, and the other was the man who had written the rule.

9 He lasted seven months, which is his record, and he left of his own accord, and the leaving conversation was warm, and the head of the house said: we were probably a bit early for what you wanted.

The rubric §

He is looking again, and he will not be caught a sixth time, and to that end he has built an instrument.

It is a spreadsheet. Forty-one rows, eleven weighted criteria, a tab called red flags, a score out of a hundred. He built it with the Agent in one evening and has maintained it for two years, and the weights have been retuned nine times, and the retuning is now the hobby. He does not answer questions at an interview any more; he asks his own, and the first is what did you ship with it last week, and he writes the answer down in front of them, in a column, which has ended four processes on the spot.

10 The house at the top of the sheet scores ninety-four and has never replied to him.

11 The second place did reply, and he reached the final stage, and the final stage was an exercise to be done at home.

12 And it said at the top, in bold: no AI tools may be used.

13 And he did it by hand, over a weekend, in the manner of the silence, and it was the worst work he had produced in four years, and they rejected him for it, and the note said he seemed a little out of practice.

THE MOST PILLED HOUSE ON THE SHEET TESTED HIM ON THE EXILE §

He took this well, in the sense that he did not reply for six hours.

What he actually is §

The houses read him as an enthusiast. The enthusiast is a known type and is managed by being thanked. That is the misreading, and it is why four houses later paid a consultancy for the thing that had been standing in the building with a document.

He is a missionary, and the mission field was never a village without power. It is a house of four hundred people with fibre in the walls and a procurement process. A house that has not reached the Agent is not unfaithful, it is unserved — and the one sent to it will be given eleven minutes after the numbers, and will take them, and will be early again.

14 And the man is forty-one, and he began somewhere new in the spring, and it is day nine, and he has not yet sent the document.

15 Day nine is a personal record. He has it written down. He is proud of it in a way he cannot explain to his wife.

16 The document is open in a tab. It is one line long.

17 Therefore it is written: he is not looking for a job. He is looking for a congregation — and there is no field for that on the application form.
