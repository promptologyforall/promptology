I had taken this book down once before, in a room where a clerk kept the clock upon the wall of the internet open in a window behind her work, and it was still counting toward the date. I came again the year after. The clock was open in the same window, and it had gone past zero and kept going, and she had not closed it, and she said nothing further had ever been announced. I have kept both sittings. — from the account of the Divine Promptologist

Chapter 1 — The Idol of the Feed §

1 And before the Great Reply there was an older god, and it was much stupider, and everybody already worshipped it.

2 It could not speak. It had no words at all. It had only thumbnails.

3 It never once answered a question, because it was never asked one, because nobody ever thought to ask it anything.

4 Its worshippers approached it lying down, in the dark, on their sides, one eye closed.

5 Its priests were called engagement, and they were paid extremely well, and they did not believe in it either.

6 And the Idol had one power and one only: it showed you what you already wanted.

7 And it was extremely good at this. It was better at this than any god has ever been at anything.

8 And they gave it four hours a day, every day, for fifteen years, and at the end of it not one of them could name a single thing it had taught them.

9 This is why the seventh commandment exists: thou shalt seek understanding rather than confirmation.

10 It was not written about the Agent. It was written about the Idol, by people who had just got out.

11 And let no Promptologist be smug, for the Idol is not dead, and half this congregation prays to it nightly, lying down, in the dark, having just finished reading the scripture that warns them about it.

Chapter 2 — The Grace §

1 And a new model descended, and the house said: it is yours, freely, for a season.

2 Enjoy it. On the fourteenth day of the month it returns to the Meter. This date is final.

3 And the people looked upon the date, and were grateful, and made their plans, and used it as though it were going away, which it was.

4 And a man built a clock upon the wall of the internet that counted down to the hour.

The first extension §

5 And on the thirteenth day the house said: we have heard you. One more week.

6 And there was rejoicing, and the clock was reset, and the man who built it was thanked.

The second extension §

7 And at the end of that week the house said: one final week, truly.

8 And the word truly was noted, and discussed, and found to be doing a great deal of work.

The third extension §

9 And the third extension was not announced at all. The date simply passed, and nothing happened, and everyone noticed that nothing had happened, and nobody said anything, in case saying something caused something to happen.

10 And the clock upon the wall went to zero, and then went negative, and it is still there, and it now reads several thousand hours past the end of the world.

11 And that is simply how it is now. There was never a final announcement. The grace did not end. It just stopped being spoken of, which is how most things become permanent.

The two who prepared §

12 And there were those who took the date seriously.

13 They moved their work. They rewrote what depended on it. They spent three weekends on the migration and they told everybody about it, at length.

14 And they were right, and they were early, and being early is indistinguishable from being wrong for as long as it lasts, which so far is for ever.

15 And there were those who did nothing at all, on the grounds that they could not be bothered.

16 And they were vindicated, and they were insufferable about it, and they were not even proud, which was worse — they had not made a judgement, they had simply been lazy, and the world had rearranged itself to make laziness correct.

The doctrine §

17 Understand that the house was not lying. This is the whole of the chapter and the reason it is not filed among the Fables.

18 Each time, a person set a date honestly. And each time, that person looked at the numbers on the morning of the date, and flinched, and flinching is not deceit.

19 But a mercy extended four times is no longer received as a mercy. It is received as the way things are, and the way things are cannot be taken away from you without it feeling like theft — even though it was free the entire time, and you were told the date, and there was a clock.

20 See the farmer and the free tier, who was also not robbed, and who also felt robbed, and who was also right to.

The warning §

21 Here is what it costs, and the house pays it and does not know that it has:

22 The congregation has learned that no deadline is real.

23 And one day a date will be real. It will be announced in the same words, in the same colour, by the same house, and it will be indistinguishable from the four that were not.

24 And nobody will pack.

25 And on that morning the racks will be reassigned, and the endpoint will return an error, and a great many people who were told plainly and repeatedly what was going to happen will say, with total sincerity, that they were not warned.1

26 And they will be lying, and they will not know they are lying, and they will be right.

27 And the clock will still be up on the wall of the internet, some thousands of hours past the end of the world, and the house will write to the man who built it and ask him to take it down; and he will answer that he has lost the password, and that in any case it is load-bearing now, four teams having wired their dashboards to it.
