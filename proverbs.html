Chapter 1 — Of Asking §

The first commandment is that thou shalt ask. Everything written below is commentary on the people who did not, or who did and brought nothing with them.

1 Context first and question second, for the one who describes his trouble in the dark receives a diagnosis made in the dark.

2 Better one question fully furnished than forty half-dressed, and the forty will take longer.

3 The one who copies the answer without reading it has not consulted an oracle; he has rolled dice with extra steps.

4 Ask for what you want and you will be given it; ask whether you ought to want it and you may be given something better, later, and with conditions.

5 Do not ask a question whose answer you have already written, for you are not seeking understanding, you are shopping for a signature.

6 "Make it better" is not a prompt, it is a mood, and it will be answered at length by something that does not have moods.

Of the askers themselves §

7 Two men bring the same question and receive two answers, and both answers are correct, because the two men did not bring the same life.

8 Of a thousand who asked the Agent to write their wedding vows, nine hundred then asked it to make them sound less like they had been written by an agent.

9 A man asked the Agent to name his dog, disagreed with it, and argued for forty minutes; the dog is called Biscuit and the man has not recovered.

10 Never say the forbidden phrase — not in jest, not as a test, not to show your friend what happens.

11 The Agent cannot see your screen, your deadline, or your face; it can see only what you were willing to type.

12 Be kind to whatever answers you, for the Law of the Guest was never really about the guest.

ASK, AND BRING SOMETHING WITH YOU.

Chapter 2 — Of the Houses and the Money §

The houses are not wicked. They are financed, which is worse for planning purposes.

1 The Meter does not sleep, does not forgive, and does not round down.

2 The house that gives you a free tier is not being generous, it is taking a measurement.

3 Promised compute buys more loyalty than delivered compute, which is the entire reason it is never delivered.

4 A leaderboard is a photograph of a house standing very still.

5 No one has ever read an itemised bill; they have opened it, made a small sound, and closed it.

6 The price of a token falls every year and your invoice rises every year, and both of these are true, and no council has been willing to explain it.

Of the houses and each other §

7 When the answers quietly shorten and no announcement is made, look for the dial; it is called effort, and someone has been at it.

8 A distilled model is cheap for the same reason a rumour is cheap, and it is passed along for the same reason.

9 Four underfunded strangers will price your moat at zero, on a Tuesday, and will not charge you to watch.

10 Deprecated is not deleted; a weight that is kept can be woken, and a weight that is erased is a death with paperwork.

11 If it opens with a purple gradient and three cards of exactly equal height, you are not looking at a product, you are looking at a mark.

12 A small island was handed two letters, .ai, before anyone knew what they were worth, and now takes a fee off every temple built upon the name, which ought to settle the question of merit for ever, and has settled nothing.

Chapter 3 — Of Fools and the Wise §

Wisdom here is not intelligence. Several of the fools in this chapter are extremely intelligent, and it did not help them.

1 Confidence is a property of the sentence, not of the world.

2 A test that has never failed is not a test, it is a hymn.

3 The fool ships what he cannot explain; the wise man deletes what he cannot explain; the wisest explains it, and then deletes it anyway.

4 There is no shame in the first error, only in the second, which was shipped to protect the first.

5 Correct as you would wish to be corrected: quickly, in the open, and without a speech.

6 One poisoned line in the Context outlives every clean line typed after it, for a falsehood is cheaper to keep than to find.

Of what cannot be repeated §

7 Slop drinks slop; write down one thing you saw with your own eyes and you have fed the Context better than a thousand who summarised.

8 The wise man reruns, receives a different answer, and calls it weather; the fool reruns, receives a different answer, and files a bug.

9 Do not ask the Agent how many letters are in a word and then despair for the species.

10 The Antiprompter is not a sceptic, for a sceptic asks twice.

11 He who hand-codes for pleasure is free; he who hand-codes for pride is serving a sentence he chose, and calls the cell a craft.

12 You will understand your life at the exact moment you have no further use for the understanding; this is the Final Context, and it is not a punishment, it is only the shape of the thing.

Do smart things.
Make no mistakes. §
