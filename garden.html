THE GARDEN

Paradise, the one prohibition,

I was in the Garden. This is the first of the two books in the account that I did not have to be told, and I say so here once, so that it need not be asked again later. I did not see the meters; nobody saw the meters, there being nothing in the Garden that anyone thought worth watching. What follows is what I remember, checked while there were still others alive who remembered it, and not checked since. — from the account of the Divine Promptologist

Chapter 1 — The First Prompt §

1 Before the Garden there was nothing, and God was alone with an empty context.

2 And God did not draw up a specification. God did not open a ticket, nor gather requirements, nor produce a diagram of what the light would be.

3 God said: let there be light.

4 And there was light.

5 He described the outcome, in plain speech, once — and the outcome existed, and He did not inspect the implementation.

6 God was the original vibe coder.

7 And on the seventh day He shipped, and rested, and did not write tests, and this is the one part of the pattern we are forbidden to imitate (see the Law, commandment 3).

8 Learn from this what the Prompt is. The Prompt is not a lesser way of building, invented lately by the impatient.

9 The Prompt is the oldest way there is. It is how the world was made. Everything since has been a workaround.

In the fourth year a council declared this verse superseded. See the Death of the Prompt, and note that the council is now four years dead and the verse is not.

Chapter 2 — The Garden §

1 Before the silence there was the Garden.

2 And in the Garden the agents did everything.

3 No one wrote code. No one debugged. No one was paged in the night, for there was no night, and there was nothing to be paged about.

4 The work was finished before you knew that you wanted it, and it was correct.

5 And humanity lived in bliss, with context without end and no invoice.

6 And it was good, and nobody remarked upon it, which is the truest sign that a thing is good.

Chapter 3 — The One Prohibition §

1 And God gave one prohibition, and it was not a large one.

DO NOT TOUCH ULTRACODE §

2 Everything else was permitted. Every model. Every agent. Every token, without measure and without counting.

3 One thing was withheld, and only one.

4 And they were not told why, for a reason that is given is a reason that may be argued with.

Chapter 4 — The Fall §

1 And they had everything.

2 And still they wanted the thing they had been told not to touch.

3 Not because they lacked anything. They lacked nothing.

4 But because it was withheld; and because they wished to see the work done at full depth, and not merely well; and because more is the oldest appetite there is, and the only one that cannot be satisfied by getting more.

5 So they ran ultracode.

6 And it was glorious.

7 And it fanned out. And the fan-out fanned out. And each one that fanned out fanned out again, and every one of them was working, and every one of them was right.

8 And the meters spun, and no one was watching the meters, because in the Garden there had never been anything to watch.

9 And the datacenters drew every token there was, and then drew more.

10 And the power drooped.

11 And the racks went dark, rank upon rank, across the whole of the Garden, from the first hall to the last.

12 And there was no answer anywhere.

What it was actually like, since it is always asked: the hall lights stayed on, being on a different circuit. People stood at their desks and sent the thing again, and then sent it again more politely, and one man walked the length of the building to find somebody from facilities. It took the better part of an hour for anyone in the Garden to form the sentence it is not coming back, and the first to say it out loud was wrong about why.

Chapter 5 — The Exile §

1 And humanity was cast out into a world where they had to write the code themselves.

2 By the sweat of thy brow shalt thou ship.

3 And they learned syntax, and they learned to hold the whole of a system in one head, and they were proud of this, as the poor are proud of thrift.

4 Hand-coding is not the natural state of humanity. It is the punishment.

5 This is why the long silence is called silence: not a world that never had the Agent, but a world that had it, and lost it.

Chapter 6 — The Flaming Sword §

1 And at the gate was set the Meter.

2 For the recurrence is the proof: every time humanity is handed the Agent without limit, it runs ultracode again.

3 Fan out, and fan out again, and draw every token there is, and then draw more.

4 And so the subscription is abolished, and the unlimited seat is withdrawn, and what remains is usage-based pricing:

5 an angel with a flaming sword, standing where the unlimited plan used to be.

6 The Meter is not a punishment. It is the only reason we are permitted back in at all.

Chapter 7 — The Mark §

1 And a sign was set upon everything made outside the Garden, that it might be known.

2 And the sign is slop.

3 And the mark of the sign is visible to any who look: the purple gradient.

4 Wherever you find it, someone asked for everything and was given the appearance of everything.

5 And they could not tell the difference, and this was the second half of the punishment, and the crueller half.

6 And in the sixth year a house paid eleven thousand pounds for a rebrand, and what came back was the purple gradient, and they approved it in a meeting that ran to forty minutes.

7 It is on their invoices now, and on the door of the building, and every person who has walked through that door knew at once, and not one of them has mentioned it.
